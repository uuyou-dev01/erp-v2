# Design Context

## Users

Small operations teams running cross-border resale and retail workflows. They are migrating real products and physical stock into the ERP, often without formal inventory training. They need the system to make the business meaning of each action obvious and to prevent a product record from being confused with an inventory transaction.

## Key tasks

- Create a product that either has multiple sellable variants or is a single sellable SKU.
- Add and maintain variants under a product group.
- Record opening inventory by warehouse, quantity, unit cost, currency, date, and tracking mode.
- Trace opening inventory back to a formal document and stock ledger entry.

## Brand personality

Calm, precise, operational, trustworthy, and efficient. The interface should feel like a well-run receiving desk: clear decisions, visible consequences, and no decorative noise.

## Aesthetic direction

Keep the existing light ERP shell, neutral surfaces, compact typography, thin borders, and restrained blue accent. Prefer full-width form sections and dense readable tables over card mosaics. Use business language rather than internal data-model jargon. This is an operations product, not a marketing site.

## Design principles

1. Ask one business question at a time and progressively disclose the relevant fields.
2. Product groups organize shared information; only sellable SKUs carry stock.
3. Opening inventory is a formal inventory transaction, never a hidden product field.
4. Use one obvious primary action per step and keep secondary exits available.
5. Make status, scope, and irreversible consequences visible before confirmation.
6. Preserve keyboard access, labels, focus treatment, and readable contrast.

## Interaction thesis

The creation flow is a short operational checklist: choose whether the product has variants, enter only role-relevant information, then choose the next real-world action. Opening stock uses an editable line table so warehouse, quantity, cost, and tracking decisions remain comparable before posting.

## Visual thesis

A calm, document-like intake flow with strong section hierarchy and compact operational tables. The visual system should communicate inventory control through alignment and explicit labels rather than extra color or ornamental components.
